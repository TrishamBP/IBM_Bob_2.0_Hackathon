# ACME Corp Employee Grievance Procedure

This procedure describes how ACME employees raise, escalate, and resolve workplace grievances. It is intended to be fair, prompt, and accessible. This is a fictional procedure and not legal advice; real-world procedures require review by qualified legal professionals.

## What Counts as a Grievance

A grievance is any concern about work conditions, treatment, decisions, or policy application that an employee wishes to formally raise. Examples include:

- Perceived unfair treatment by a manager or peer.
- Disagreement with a performance rating or promotion decision.
- Concerns about workload, role clarity, or career growth.
- Concerns about workplace conduct that do not meet the threshold of [`01-hr/anti-harassment-policy.md`](./anti-harassment-policy.md).
- Concerns about policy application (e.g., leave, benefits, equipment).

Harassment and discrimination should be raised directly through the [`01-hr/anti-harassment-policy.md`](./anti-harassment-policy.md) channels. Security concerns should be raised through [`03-security/security-incident-reporting.md`](../03-security/security-incident-reporting.md).

## Step 1 — Informal Resolution

Most grievances are best resolved informally with the person concerned. Where possible, raise the issue directly and in good faith. If you cannot raise it directly, or the direct conversation did not resolve it, proceed to Step 2.

## Step 2 — Formal Grievance

1. Submit a written grievance to your HRBP (see [`09-contacts/contact-directory.md`](../09-contacts/contact-directory.md)). Use [`08-forms/policy-acknowledgement.md`](../08-forms/policy-acknowledgement.md) as a starting template for the write-up (the form is also used for acknowledgements, but the structure is useful).
2. The HRBP acknowledges receipt within 1 business day.
3. The HRBP investigates by speaking with the concerned parties and reviewing relevant evidence (emails, Teams transcripts, performance records).
4. The HRBP provides a written response within 10 business days, with proposed resolution steps.

## Step 3 — Escalation

If the Step 2 resolution is not satisfactory:

1. Escalate in writing to the HR Director for your office (see [`00-company/organizational-structure.md`](../00-company/organizational-structure.md)).
2. The HR Director acknowledges within 1 business day and provides a written response within 15 business days.
3. The HR Director may convene a small panel (HR Director, the relevant VP, and a peer representative) to review the grievance.

## Step 4 — Final Escalation

If the Step 3 outcome is still not satisfactory:

1. Escalate in writing to the VP HR (`ananya.sharma@acme.example`).
2. The VP HR reviews and responds within 15 business days.
3. The VP HR's response is the final internal escalation.

## Confidentiality and Non-Retaliation

- Grievance information is shared on a need-to-know basis.
- Retaliation against an employee for raising a grievance is itself a disciplinary matter.
- Employees who raise grievances in good faith will not face adverse employment consequences for raising them.

## External Recourse

Nothing in this procedure prevents an employee from seeking remedies available under applicable law, including approaching the labor court or relevant statutory body.
